Physarum polycephalum on a map of Berlin

Schleimpilz

In 2010, researchers put oat flakes on a map of the Tokyo area, one for each big city, and let a slime mold loose on it. The network it grew looked a lot like the region’s railways. This dish runs the same experiment on Berlin. The slime starts at CODE University in Neukölln, and there’s an oat flake on 37 of the city’s busiest stations.

    After Tero et al., “Rules for Biologically Inspired Adaptive Network Design”, Science 327 (2010). Tubes carry protoplasm between the oat flakes. A tube that carries a lot grows thicker, one that carries little withers.

    Outside the city the dish is lit, the way the researchers lit the sea and the mountains around Tokyo. Places more than 500 m from any bus, tram or train stop get a dimmer light. The slime can cross them but would rather not.
